## Full speech model and archive reset: October 5

At the owner's request, production switched to full-precision `large-v3` and started a fresh voice/text archive on **2026-10-05 at 13:41 CEST**. The bot release at model cutover was `6eec33355de4f1df0f8279efc40c3af3c549278f`; this change upgraded only the separately managed speech worker and its resource allowance. The subsequent asset release above preserved that worker. The default GPU build/Compose configuration now records the same model, revision and precision. The explicit CPU alternative retains its pinned `base.en` model. The test instance remains on its previous model and data.

The exact candidate passed all seven worker tests and real GPU requests for an 11-second public passage, three-second excerpts, 20-second chunks and silence. Peak total GPU use was **7,244 MiB**, including the two existing base-model workers. Peak container RAM was **6,250,614,784 bytes** during loading, with about 1.5 GiB remaining after the probe. Warm 11-second requests took 1.74–1.80 seconds; 20-second requests took 2.45–2.55 seconds. The passage matched its 22-word reference after punctuation/case normalization, and silence produced no text. This is a small compatibility and performance check, not a general word-accuracy guarantee. Production's actual worker also matched the public reference before capture resumed.

TrueNAS stop job **234234** completed and backup **234238** restored successfully before the reset. The first worker update, **234240**, failed because the previous 1 GiB no-swap limit was smaller than the new RAM limit; no archive deletion had occurred. Import checkpoints and cached channel permissions were cleared; the bot rebuilt current permissions on startup. A deletion fence at `2026-10-05T11:41:05.728052Z` prevents old file replay from resurrecting pre-reset records. Settings, channel exclusions, indefinite retention and read-access policies remain in place. Fresh capture has resumed. No raw microphone audio is retained.

At 13:55 CEST, the fresh archive contained 28 events, including three speech records all marked `large-v3` and eleven chat posts; no event predated the deletion fence. All 13 channel-permission snapshots were fresh and row security remained enabled. The worker was healthy with no restarts or out-of-memory kills, and the bot's archive was synced. Their underlying errors were not retained by the existing generic failure handler, so this deployment does not claim those failures are fixed or that microphone accuracy is established.
